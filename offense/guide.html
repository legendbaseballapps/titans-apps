Offense Chart Coach's Guide

How to chart the other team's pitcher while we hit. Read it once before you go, then keep it open on your phone for the first inning.

App v43  ·  October 2026  ·  Game day

What it does

  • Records every pitch they throw us: what it was, where it crossed, and what our hitter did with it.
  • Builds a scouting read on their pitcher as the game goes — his mix, what he goes to when he's ahead, and where he lives in the zone.
  • Shows how our hitters are being attacked: chase rate, first-pitch swings, whiffs, where we're hitting it.
  • Maps the umpire's zone from the pitches we took, so by the third inning you know what we're actually working with.
  • Keeps every game. The next time we see this pitcher, you can pull up what he did to us last time.
This one is different

Unlike the pitch chart and the bullpen chart, this app never overwrites a game. Filing a game moves it to History. An unexported game is still recoverable weeks later.

Getting it on the iPad

Do this at home, on wifi. Once it is on the home screen it opens with no signal at all.

  • Open legendbaseballapps.github.io/titans-apps in Safari. Not Chrome, and not the browser inside Messages or Mail — if it opens in one of those, tap the compass icon to send it to Safari.
  • Tap Offense Chart.
  • Wait for the green bar that says “Ready to use offline.” That is the app saving a copy. Don't skip it.
  • Share → Add to Home Screen. Name it Offense. You get the bat-and-plate icon.
  • Prove it: turn on Airplane Mode, close Safari, tap the new icon. It should open normally.
  • Settings → Display & Brightness → Auto-Lock → Never. Chart in landscape.
Which version am I on?

The build is printed in the header, right after “Offense Chart.” This guide describes v43. If it shows two numbers in amber, like v28 ≠ v27, that iPad downloaded an update but is still showing the old one — see below.

Getting a new version

You set the icon up once. It never has to be replaced — the app replaces itself underneath it.

  1. Open the app once on wifi. The new copy downloads quietly in the background; the screen still shows the old one.
  2. Close it completely from the app switcher — swipe up, then flick the app away.
  3. Open it again. New version. Check the build in the header.
Don't delete the icon and add it back

On iOS, removing a home-screen app can take its saved data with it — and this app is holding every game you've charted, not just today's. Export from History before you ever remove it.

Set it up once, in February

The roster is released, not typed

The roster ships with the app. You can read it on the Setup tab; you cannot change it. The same released file backs this app and the Bullpen Chart, so a player is one player across both — the Pitch Chart isn't wired in yet and still types our pitchers free-form.

Why. Every export joins on an ID. When each iPad kept its own roster, the same kid typed on two iPads got two different IDs and his season quietly split in two the moment the files were stacked up. One released list is the fix: Jim maintains it, every existing ID comes forward with each release, and it arrives with the app update. Anything you had typed in here is still shown, labelled as a leftover local list, until this iPad picks up the update.

Somebody wrong, missing or graduated? Tell Jim. Nothing already charted is disturbed — a filed game keeps the names it was charted under.

A corrected roster does not need an app update. Open the app once with signal and it picks up the current roster on its own. With no signal it keeps the one it has, and the banner says which that is.

Two new columns: Class (Fr/So/Jr/Sr) and Throws, which is separate from Bats because plenty of kids throw right and hit left. The bullpen needs the arm, this app needs the bat, and class is what lets a pitcher be compared against his own class rather than the whole program.

On the Setup tab.

  • Titans roster — the banner says which roster this iPad has; the player list is folded away behind Show all … and opens by itself if anything is wrong. Inside: last and first name, the label the app will actually print, number, class, and which way he bats and throws. A hitter reads as just his last name unless somebody shares it, in which case he becomes Dieker, T automatically. SAMPLE roster — not the real team in red means the real one hasn't been released yet and these are placeholders; it will let you chart, which is why it shouts. Stop and tell Jim.
  • Our lineup — pick the order from the roster. The app opens on whatever order you used last game, so most days you only change what moved.

Two minutes before first pitch

  • Fill in the date and opponent across the top, and who's charting on the Setup tab.
  • Set the lineup if it changed.
  • Enter their starter — name or number is enough, plus whether he throws R or L.
  • Seen him before? Tap Pull on his panel and take him off the list — see below.
  • Nothing to set up for his pitches — the buttons are the same six every time.
It opens ready to chart

No sample game to clear out any more — it opens on a clean game with last game's batting order already in it, so most days you change what moved and start. Everything you tap is saved from the first pitch.

Scouting the man on the mound

Every pitcher's panel on the Setup tab has a row of tags and a notes box. Seven tags, all of them things you want to know walking up there the second time:

  • POUNDS — fills up the zone, be ready early
  • WILD — behind a lot, make him prove it
  • FPS — first-pitch strike guy, he starts you 0-1
  • BB-STR — lands the breaking ball for a strike, can't just sit fastball
  • NO GIVE — won't give in with two strikes
  • RUNNABLE — slow to the plate, we can run
  • QUICK — slide-steps and holds runners

POUNDS, WILD and FPS the app works out for itself once there are enough pitches charted, and marks them with a dot so you know nobody set them by hand. Tap one to overrule it — off, then on as yours, then back to the app. Your tap always wins.

POUNDS and WILD are opposites. Turning one on turns the other off.

Notes are yours and free-form. Both tags and notes go out in the export, with a * on anything the app detected rather than a coach deciding.

Pulling him off the list

We play most teams twice. The second time, tap Pull on his panel and pick him out of the list of everybody we've already charted. His hand, number, your notes and your tags come back with him.

Nothing from the pitch data is copied — today starts clean, and the panel says “Reads carried in from Northview, 4/18” so nobody mistakes a note from six weeks ago for something somebody saw this afternoon. Check them against what he's actually doing.

Only his most recent look comes across. A note from April beats the same note from February.

Charting a pitch

Two taps for most pitches. Three when we swing and miss, four when we put it in play.

1 · Pitch type

Tap what he threw. It stays set, so a pitcher working off his fastball is one tap per pitch.

Six buttons, the same for every pitcher, every game:

  • FB — fastball (two-seam or four, it doesn't split them)
  • CT — cutter
  • CB — curveball
  • SL — slider
  • CH — change-up
  • SP — splitter
Why you can't add your own

These six cover nearly everything a high-school arm throws, and a fixed list is what lets a pitcher's numbers add up across games. If one charter types “SL” and the next types “Slider,” the app has two pitches instead of one and the count grid splits in half. If you genuinely can't tell what it was, put it in the closest bucket and keep moving — one mislabelled pitch costs far less than a split column.

2 · Location

Catcher's view, same grid as the other two apps. The shaded middle nine are the strike zone; the outer ring is off the plate. Tap where it crossed.

That tap logs the pitch as taken — ball or strike by where you put it. For a pitch our hitter watched go by, you're done in two taps.

3 · What happened

Three buttons, and they are the three things that keep the at-bat going: Took it, Foul, Swing & miss.

If the at-bat is over, skip this step entirely. A ball in play and a hit batter both end it, and both are already in the result list below — so tap the result and the app works out the rest. There is no In play button any more and there doesn't need to be: anything that isn't a K, a walk or a hit batter is a ball in play.

The default is “took it”

If you plot a pitch and tap nothing else, it records as taken. That's deliberate — most pitches are. But it means a missed tap shows up as a take, so glance at the pitch strip under the grid: every pitch is listed with the count it came in and what it did.

What is coming next

Under the pitch strip there is a What is coming line. It reads the count the hitter is standing in right now and tells you the most likely next pitch and where it usually goes — built from this game plus every filed game against the same pitcher, on this hitter's side of the plate.

When the at-bat ends it switches to the next hitter's first pitch, so you have something to tell him on his way up.

It will often say nothing

It needs four pitches in that exact count before it will call anything, and it prints the raw number every time — 6 of 7 means six of the seven pitches he has thrown in this count were that pitch. The first time you see a pitcher it will be blank most of the day. That is correct. It fills in the second and third time you face him.

When the ump disagrees

On any taken pitch an Umpire called it row appears, showing what the app assumed. If he rang up a pitch off the plate, or gave our hitter one in the zone, tap to flip it. The count corrects itself.

That one tap is the whole umpire report. Nothing else to do.

Ending the at-bat

  • Strikeouts and walks close themselves. Three strikes and it's K, or Kc if he took it. Four balls and it's BB.
  • Everything else: tap the result — 1B, 2B, GO, FO and the rest.
  • On a ball in play, tap how we hit it (ground, line, fly, pop) and then tap the field where it went. The line that draws tells you the direction and how far.
  • The diamond has the fielding positions printed on it — 1 through 9, scorer's numbering. They are there to aim at: "the shortstop fielded it" is far easier to place than eyeballing a blank wedge, and it keeps two charters marking the same ball in the same spot. The Report tab's team spray leaves them off, since nobody taps that one.
  • How we hit it is always on screen, and tapping it also tells the app the ball was put in play. So does tapping the result. Either order works — result first or trajectory first.
  • Most results fill it in for you. GO, FO, LO, PO, FC, SAC, SF, DP and HR each say how the ball was hit, so the app ticks the box and you move on. The four it cannot work out — 1B, 2B, 3B and E — could be any of the four, so it asks: the heading turns amber and reads How did we hit it? until you answer. If you never do, the ball still counts and still plots on the field; it just sits out of the ground/line/fly mix.
  • Hard contact is a separate tap, and it is yours to make. The app never guesses it — a scorched grounder at the third baseman sits in the same spot on the field as a roller, so only the person watching can tell. Tap Hard contact when we squared one up; leave it alone for everything from weak to normal. It is what colours the spray chart, so what we did to the ball leads rather than whether it happened to find grass.
  • SAC is the sacrifice bunt; SF is the sacrifice fly. New in v43. Both count as a quality at-bat, so pick the right one.
  • On this play: Run scored and Moved a runner. New in v43, for quality at-bats. They show up under Hard contact once the ball is in play. Tap Run scored when any run scores on his ball in play: a hit, an out, an error, a fielder's choice or a double play all count. Tap Moved a runner when he moves a runner into scoring position with fewer than 2 outs, even if he is out. The app can't see runners or the scoreboard, so an untapped chip means it didn't happen. Tap them every time, before Next hitter.
  • Under the chips, one line tells you whether the at-bat counts: Quality at-bat · moved a runner, 2-strike contact, or Not a quality at-bat. You don't decide it; the app works it out.
  • Reached on an error is a ball in play like any other. Tap E, tap how we hit it, tap the field. It counts in the contact and direction numbers but not as a hit — on the field diagram it draws as an open circle, the same as an out.
  • The field diagram shows this hitter's balls in play. Flip it to Whole team if you want the whole game at once — the Report tab always shows the team.
  • Tap Next hitter. The order advances on its own and wraps around.
Fixing something

Undo last pitch backs out one pitch, and it works after the app has closed the at-bat. Three strikes and it says K — undo, and the count goes back to 0–2 with the K gone, ready for you to plot the pitch again.

If the only thing wrong is what the pitch did, you don't need undo at all. Tap the result, or tap how we hit it, and the app re-reads the count from there — the pitch's type and location stay as you charted them.

To look at an earlier at-bat, tap that spot in the order on the left. The app says Reviewing in amber, and nothing you tap — undo included — changes anything until you tap Back to the plate. An at-bat is frozen once you tap Next hitter, so fix it before you move on.

Quality at-bats

New in v43. The program's definition, not GameChanger's. GC has its own QAB stat with different rules, so the two numbers won't match.

An at-bat is a quality at-bat when any of these happen:

  • A hit
  • A line drive, even if it's caught
  • A walk, or hit by pitch
  • A sacrifice bunt or sacrifice fly
  • A run scores on his ball in play (you tap Run scored)
  • 7 or more pitches
  • He moves a runner into scoring position with fewer than 2 outs (you tap Moved a runner)
  • He puts the ball in play with 2 strikes

Reaching on an error doesn't count by itself. It still counts if another rule fires, for example with 2 strikes or when a run scores on it.

7 of these the app already knows from what you chart. The other 2 need your tap, which is why the chips matter.

Pitching changes

Tap + Reliever at the top of the Chart tab. Name him, and everything from that pitch forward is charted against him. Tap a pitcher's chip to switch back if you need to.

Each pitcher's report is separate, which is the point — the starter's mix and the closer's mix are different scouting problems.

What the report tells you

On the Report tab, updating as you chart.

  • When they have used more than one pitcher, a row of pitcher chips sits at the top. The report shows whoever is on the mound; tap another to look back at the starter. Changing pitchers on the Chart tab snaps it back to the man pitching.
  • Our hitters, the umpire and where we hit it live on their own At the plate chip, next to the pitchers — they cover the whole game, not one pitcher. One tap instead of a long scroll.
  • Strike % and first-pitch strike % — is he around the plate, and does he start hitters off with a strike? If first-pitch strike is low, we can take.
  • Our chase % — how often we swing at pitches off the plate. This is the number that wins or loses the game, and it's the one to tell the dugout.
  • What he goes to, by count — his mix on the first pitch, when he's ahead, even, behind, and with two strikes. If he throws a breaking ball with two strikes 80% of the time, our hitters should know that by the fourth inning.
  • Where he puts it — a heat grid per pitch. The fastball lives up, the slider lives away, and so on.
  • Quality at-bats, new in v43: how many of our at-bats were quality at-bats, and a table of how they got there. One at-bat can count more than one way, so the rows add up to more than the total.
  • Our hitters — pitches per plate appearance, first-pitch swings, quality at-bats, chase, whiffs, line drives.
  • The umpire — red cells are strikes called off the plate, green are pitches in the zone he gave us, and next to the map it tells you in words: “He is giving the corner away — 4 of 5 pitches taken off the plate away came back a strike.” That is the sentence to pass to the hitters.
  • Where we hit it — a filled dot is a hit, an open ring is an out; a dashed line is a ground ball and an arc is in the air. Beside it, how we are spreading the ball: pull / center / oppo, and ground / line / fly.

End of the game

  • Tap Export in the header. One row per pitch, opens in Excel or Numbers.
  • Then tap Finish & file this game on Setup. It moves to History and a clean game starts.
If you forget, nothing is lost

Filing never deletes. Even if you never export, every game you charted is sitting in History with an Export button next to it. If more than one game is sitting there unsent, the app says so at the top.

Erase everything on this iPad

At the bottom of Setup there is a red Erase everything on this iPad button. It deletes the game in progress and everything in History, for good, with nothing uploaded first. It is there for testing — charting a few junk games and wanting a clean app back.

It tells you exactly what it is about to destroy, including how many filed games have never been exported, and Cancel is what the button starts on. Finish & file is how a real game ends; this is not. The roster is not affected — it ships with the app.

The next time we see him

The History tab has a Pitchers we have seen table built from every filed game — how many times we've faced him, his strike percentage, how often we whiff, and what he throws.

Spell his name the same way each time and it all lands on one line. If you only have a number, use the number consistently. That spelling is also what the Pull button matches on, on the Setup tab.

Your tags and notes show up here too

Pick him off the name chips and his tags sit right on the WHAT TO EXPECT header, next to his name — because they are what to expect. Your notes and where they were last written down sit on the line just under it. The Reads column in the table below does the same thing at a glance for everybody.

Dotted tags are the ones the app worked out from the pitches; solid ones are somebody's call.

The same header shows up on the Report tab during a game, for whoever is on the mound right now.

It is read-only in both places. They are for reading — the night before, or between innings. To change a tag or a note, do it on the Setup tab, so there is never a question of which copy is current.

Things it doesn't do

  • It does not track velocity. If you have a radar, write it down.
  • It does not know balls and strikes from the scoreboard — only from what you chart.
  • It does not track runners, outs or innings. This is a pitcher-and-hitter chart, not a scorebook. The two quality at-bat chips are the only runner facts it records, and only because you tap them.
  • It does not upload anything on its own yet. Export, or hand the iPad to Jim.

What to send back

Chart a full game if you can, or three innings if that's what you get. Then tell me:

  • Whether two-to-three taps keeps up, or whether you fell behind and started missing pitches.
  • Any pitch that landed on the wrong hitter, and what you were doing when it happened.
  • Whether the pitch-type buttons matched what he actually threw, or whether you were guessing.
  • Whether the chase number told you anything you could use during the game.
  • What you ended up writing on paper anyway. That one matters most.
