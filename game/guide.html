Pitch Chart Coach's Guide

Everything you need to chart a game on the iPad. Read it once before you go, then keep it open on your phone for the first inning.

App v33  ·  October 2026  ·  Game day

What it does

  • Records every pitch: type, where it crossed the plate, and what the hitter did with it.
  • Keeps a running count, strike percentage, first-pitch strike percentage and AO3+ for each pitcher.
  • Builds a scouting read on every opposing hitter as the game goes, so the second and third time through you can see how you've pitched him.
  • Exports the whole game as text you can paste into a spreadsheet.
Before you start

It charts one game at a time, and filing a game never deletes it — it moves to History, where it keeps an Export button for as long as you want it. Export anyway; History is the safety net, not the delivery.

Getting it on the iPad

Do this at home, on wifi. Once it is on the home screen it opens with no signal at all — which matters, because you cannot count on reception at the field.

  • Open legendbaseballapps.github.io/titans-apps in Safari. Not Chrome, and not the browser inside Messages or Mail — if it opens in one of those, tap the compass icon to send it to Safari.
  • Tap Pitch Chart.
  • Wait for the green bar that says “Ready to use offline.” That is the app telling you it has saved a copy. Don't skip it.
  • Share → Add to Home Screen. Name it Pitch Chart. You get the Titans field icon.
  • Prove it: turn on Airplane Mode, close Safari, tap the new icon. It should open normally.
  • Settings → Display & Brightness → Auto-Lock → Never. The app also asks iOS to hold the screen on; this is the backup for when iOS says no.
  • Chart in landscape. The page scrolls up and down, never sideways. Portrait puts the result buttons below the fold.
Which version am I on?

The build is printed in the header, right after “Pitch Chart.” This guide describes v33. If it shows two numbers in amber, like v15 ≠ v14, that iPad has downloaded an update but is still showing the old one — see below.

Getting a new version

You set the icon up once. It never has to be replaced — the app replaces itself underneath it.

  1. Open the app once on wifi. The new copy downloads quietly in the background; the screen still shows the old one.
  2. Close it completely from the app switcher — swipe up, then flick the app away.
  3. Open it again. New version. Check the build in the header.
Don't delete the icon and add it back

That is the one way to lose data. On iOS, removing a home-screen app can take its saved game with it. The two steps above are all an update needs. If you ever do have to remove it, export first.

Before the first pitch

  • Finish Setup before the first pitch. New in v29. The app opens on the Setup tab every game and won't chart a pitch until Setup is done: the level, the date, the opponent, the starting pitcher, and a name and jersey number for every hitter in their lineup. The Ready to chart box at the bottom of Setup lists whatever is still missing, turns green when it's all in, and its Start charting button takes you to the Chart tab. Before then, the Chart tab opens to a Not ready to chart yet screen (Still needed: Complete Setup) with a Back to Setup button, the same in all 3 apps. Once the game is under way the lock is off, so a sub with no name yet never stops you.
  • Pick the level: Varsity, JV, Sophomore or Freshman. It starts blank every game. It's what puts the game in the right level on the dashboard. Picked the wrong one? Tap the Setup tab and tap the right one any time before you file.
  • All 3 apps now work alike (Oct 2026): the same header (Date and Opponent on the left; Setup, Chart, Report, History; then Guide and Finish & file), a This game panel on Setup with who's charting and the practice switch, a Ready to chart box, Start over at the bottom of Setup, Export at the bottom of Report, and one pitch-type order everywhere: FB CT SL CB CH SP.
  • 4 tabs across the top, new in v28, in the same place and order as the Offense Chart: Setup, Chart, Report, History. Guide and Finish & file sit to their right. The pitcher chips and the live numbers show only on the Chart tab.
  • Training a new charter, or just trying the app out? Tick Practice game in the This game panel. It charts and files like any game but is never sent to the dashboard, and a bar above the chart reminds you it's on. It switches itself off when you file.
  • The date at the top starts on today (tap it for the calendar); type the opponent beside it. Under the level, the This game panel has Who's charting, Home or away and the practice switch. Then pick your starter under Our pitchers on Setup. That is the only place he is set (v30): on the Chart tab his chip shows his name and pitch count, and tapping it makes him the pitcher on the mound, but it can't change who started. Relievers go on with + Reliever on the Chart tab as they come in, each picked from the roster on his own chip. Nothing to type, and the starter carries over to the next game.
  • Type the opposing lineup on Setup: name, jersey, position, and R or L. Position is optional; name and jersey are required.
  • Position is a drop-down and it is the scorecard number — 1 pitcher through 9 right field, plus DH. The abbreviation is printed beside each number so you are not counting on your fingers. It had to be numbers: 1B, 2B and 3B are already results on the pad two inches away.
  • The lineup defaults to 9 spots. Use the minus and plus at the bottom to go up to 12 if they bat a longer order. A spot that already has a name or a pitch in it won't drop.
  • Tap ◄ Lineup in the header once the lineup is in. It collapses the left column and gives the charting side more room.
One jersey, one position — unless you say otherwise

Put a number on two spots and the app puts it back and tells you which spot already has it. Enter it again and it stays. That is deliberate: a sub really does come in wearing a number that is already on the card, and the app is not going to stop you charting him. Both spots then show that cell in amber so you can see at a glance what you agreed to. Positions work exactly the same way. A blank cell never collides with a blank cell.

Use the return key

Return walks you forward: date, opponent, then every lineup field top to bottom. Type, hit return, keep typing. The key says next until the last field and then done, which drops the keyboard. The lineup is the only place left that takes typing — everything else is a tap. Nothing needs to be “saved” on the way — every keystroke is already written.

Our pitchers come off the roster

New in v18. The mound chips at the top of the chart screen are drop-downs, not name fields.

  • Tap the chip, pick the arm. His class and which way he throws come with him — nothing to type, nothing to spell.
  • A pitcher already on another chip drops off the list, so the same kid can't be charted twice in one game.
  • Somebody wrong, missing or graduated? Tell Jim. Nothing already charted is disturbed — a pitcher taken off a later roster keeps his name on the chip and keeps his pitches.
  • A corrected roster does not need an app update. Open the app once with signal and it picks up the current roster on its own. With no signal it keeps the one it has.

The Setup screen has an Our pitchers panel at the top that says which roster this iPad is holding. It is a read-out, not a form.

  • Released roster in green — the real team, and the same list the bullpen chart and the offense chart are using. This is what you want.
  • SAMPLE roster — not the real team in red — placeholders. It will let you chart, which is why it shouts. Stop and tell Jim.
  • Leftover roster from another app in amber — this iPad is behind. Get it on the current version before the next game.
  • No roster in red — there is nobody to put on the mound. Reload with signal; tell Jim if it stays empty.
Why it matters

The roster is what makes a pitcher one pitcher across all three apps. The export now carries his ID alongside his name, so the bullpen work he did in January and the innings he throws in April land on the same kid — no matching spellings by hand.

Their hitters are still typed

The opposing lineup is typed, the same as always. They are not our players and never will be on our roster.

Charting a pitch

Three taps, always in this order — two on a fastball-heavy inning, because the pitch type stays where you left it. The called spot is a fourth, and it is optional.

1

Pitch type

Only when it changes. Whatever you pick stays selected until you pick something else.

FBCTSLCBCHSP
2

Called spot — optional, before the pitch

The same twenty-five boxes as the location grid, sitting beside it at the same size: what you asked for on the left, what he did on the right. The full grid, because a waste pitch or one buried off the plate is a real call. The middle nine are tinted grey here and blue over there — that is how you tell the two grids apart at a glance. Tap what you called while the pitcher is getting the sign, not after the pitch. The app then compares it to where the ball actually crossed and grades it Spot, Close or Miss, the same three buckets the bullpen chart uses, so what you build in January can finally be compared to April.

Skip it whenever you need to. A pitch with no called spot is charted normally; it just sits out of the execution numbers rather than being guessed at. Tap the same box again to clear it.

3

Location

Tap the grid where the ball crossed. It's the catcher's view, so a right-handed hitter stands on the left side of the grid. The shaded middle 9 boxes are the strike zone and the ring around them is everything off the plate.

4

What happened

Three buttons, and they are the three things that keep the at-bat going.

Took ItFoulSwing & Miss

If the at-bat is over, skip this step. A ball in play and a hit batter are already in the result list, so tap the result and the app works the rest out. There is no In Play button any more and there doesn't need to be.

Strikeouts and walks close themselves. Three strikes and it says K, or Kc if he took it. Four balls and it says BB. The hitter does not change on its own — you still tap Next batter, so there is always time to mark the spray and the scouting tags. Undo reopens it if the app got it wrong.

Location sets the call

The colours on both grids mean what the pitch did — red a strike, green a ball, navy a foul, amber in play — and the key sits right under the grid. They have never meant pitch type. The type is the small letters on each marker: 3 CB is the third pitch of the at-bat, a curveball. The same number appears on both grids and on the sequence chips below, so you can read one pitch straight across: called there, went there, did that.

When four or more pitches land in the same box the type drops off and you get bare numbers, and past three you get a grey +2 counting the rest — the full list is always in the sequence chips.

Hard contact is yours to judge. The app never guesses it — a scorched grounder at the third baseman sits where a roller sits. Tap it when we got jammed or he squared one up; leave it for everything from weak to normal. It colours the spray chart, and it works the same way in the Offense Chart.

A taken pitch inside the middle 9 boxes counts as a strike, and anything outside counts as a ball. There is no way to record a pitch the umpire got wrong, so strike percentage measures where the ball crossed.

The called spot never touches the count. Ball, strike and everything built on them come from the big grid — where the pitch actually went. A fastball called on the corner and thrown two feet outside is a ball, and it is charted as a miss. If the call set the count, the app would be grading the bench and calling it the pitcher.

End of the game

The same ritual in all three apps. Learn it once.

  • If the game ended mid-inning — walk-off, run rule, time limit — filing asks about that last inning first, headed Final inning (partial). Answer it and the file goes through; cancel and nothing is filed.
  • Finish & file is the orange button in the header from v26, there as soon as a pitch is charted, on every tab. It is the only Finish & file button, the same as the other 2 apps. It moves the game to History and starts a clean one — your pitching staff carries over, the opposing lineup does not, because next week it is a different team.
  • Filing sends the game to the dashboard as soon as there is signal, and brings back the Setup screen with the level blank for the next game.
  • Filing never deletes. Everything you have ever filed stays in History, with an Export beside it.
  • Clear pitches and Start over, new in v31, sit at the bottom of the Setup tab, the same pair in all 3 apps. Both ask first and say how many pitches go; neither touches anything in History. Clear pitches keeps everything on Setup (level, date, opponent, lineup, starter, practice switch) and clears everything charted, so you can chart the same game again. Handy in practice mode. Start over takes Setup back to blank (the starter stays). A real game ends with Finish & file, never these.
The red Erase everything button is gone

It was there for testing in the fall. From v25 it is switched off, so nothing on this iPad can wipe History.

Sending to the dashboard

New in v25. Every filed game goes to the coaching dashboard by itself. You don't tap anything, and charting never waits for it.

  • When it sends: the moment you file, and again every time the app opens or comes back to the front with signal. No signal at the field? It goes when the iPad next sees wifi.
  • One line at the top of History tells you where things stand: Every filed game is on the dashboard, or 1 game waiting to upload.
  • Each filed game has a pill: on dashboard, waiting, practice (never sent), or kept here (filed before v25, before games had a level, so it stays on the iPad).
  • Nothing is lost by waiting. A game stays in History whether it has gone or not, and sending the same one twice is harmless.
If the line turns red

It says the dashboard turned a game away, with the reason. Your games are safe on the iPad. Tell Jim, and use Export as a backup until he says it's fixed.

Ending the at-bat

  • Tap the result on the right pad. 17 options, from K and BB through DP and SAC.
  • Every at-bat needs a pitch on the zone before it can end. New in v24. The result buttons, the spray chart and Next Hitter all wait until at least one pitch is logged, so an at-bat can never be saved with a result and no pitch behind it.
  • IBB is the one exception: the intentional walk. When the coach asks for the walk without a pitch, tap IBB and then Next Hitter. It counts as a walk on the pitcher's line, and goes to the pitcher and inning on screen when you tap it. If he threw a pitch or two first, chart them, then tap IBB. Tapped it by mistake? Undo takes it back.
  • For anything he hit, tap the spray chart where the ball ended up. Hits and errors also want a trajectory: GB, LD, FB or PU. Everything else fills its own trajectory in.
  • Tap Next Hitter. It turns gold once a result is in, so a gold button means the app is waiting on you.
  • Tap the out dots in the count bar as outs happen. The app has no idea what inning it is.
  • When you change pitchers, tap + Reliever at the top, pick him off the drop-down, then tap his chip to make him the man on the mound. Nothing detects a pitching change.
Nothing moves on its own

Strike three and ball four turn the count red and flag it, and the at-bat still stays open until you pick the result. The hitter only changes when you tap Next Hitter. That's on purpose, so a ball in play never advances past the spray chart.

Innings, runs and earned runs

New in v19. The app used to know nothing about innings and you typed IP, R and ER off the scorebook on the drive home. Now you answer one question per inning, while you still remember it, and the app works the rest out.

  • The outs count themselves. Tap K, GO, FO, LO, PO, FC or SAC on the result pad and a dot lights up. DP lights two. You do not have to do anything else.
  • The dots are still yours when it gets it wrong. They sit in the count bar beside the inning number. Tap dot 1, 2 or 3 to set the count directly; tapping the dot that already is the count takes it back one. Undoing a result takes its out back off too.
  • The third out stops the app and asks about the inning — after you tap Next Hitter. Finish the at-bat first: the spray chart and the trajectory are still yours. The prompt comes when you move off the hitter. (Tapping the third out dot by hand opens it straight away, because that is you saying the inning is over rather than saying what one at-bat did.) You cannot chart past it. That is on purpose — the number is easy to get right now and impossible to reconstruct in the car.
  • Scoreless inning: one tap. NO RUNS and you are back in the game, on the next inning, outs cleared. No confirm.
  • Runs: tap the number. Earned fills in to match, because most runs are earned. If one of them was not, tap the lower number on the Earned row and confirm. Earned can never be more than runs — those buttons go grey.
  • More than one pitcher threw that inning? You get a row per arm with plus and minus on each, and the app builds that list itself from who actually threw. The inning total underneath is the sum of your rows; there is nothing to total up yourself.
  • The inning number has its own plus and minus in the count bar. You should never need it — confirming an inning rolls it — but if the app and the scoreboard disagree, that is how you line them up.
Two results that do not always mean an out

The app counts an out for all nine out results, which is right nearly every time. Two of them can be worth nothing, and it cannot tell:

A dropped third strike. You tap K, the catcher does not hold it, the batter reaches. It is still a strikeout for the pitcher — leave the result alone and tap the lit dot to take the out back off. He keeps the K and the AO3+ credit; only the inning’s out count changes.

A fielder’s choice where nobody is retired. The fielder goes for the lead runner and does not get him. Same fix: tap the dot back.

Everything else is honest — a muffed grounder or a dropped fly is scored E, not GO or FO, so those really did produce the out they name.

Ended an inning by mistake?

Wrong — back to 2 outs is the left button on that prompt. It puts the outs back to two, leaves the inning where it was, and writes nothing. Tapping outside the box or hitting escape does the same. You can never get stuck in it.

Our number and GameChanger's will not always match

Official scoring charges a run to whoever let the runner reach, which with a pitching change is a bookkeeping rule being applied by whoever has the iPad in the stands. You watched it happen. So charge the run to the pitcher you would charge it to, and do not go back and “fix” it to match the box score. GameChanger stays the official record — eligibility, roster-page ERA, end-of-season stats. This app's number drives our analysis. Nobody reconciles the two, and a difference is not a bug.

Fixing something

  • Wrong on the last pitch: tap Undo last pitch. Once for a result, again to drop the pitch itself.
  • Wrong pitch type: tap that pitch in the at-bat strip under the zone to cycle it.
  • Wrong result on the hitter before: tap ◂ Go back on the bar under the count. It stays there through the whole next at-bat, so you have time to notice.
  • Something further back: tap that hitter in the lineup, then the numbered AB chips next to his name to open the at-bat you want. If that spot has had a sub, pick the man first with the In chips.
  • Any time you're looking at an old at-bat the panel dims and a banner says so. Zone taps won't log a pitch there, and it tells you why in red rather than doing nothing. Tap Back to the plate to carry on.

Pinch hitters and substitutions

A lineup spot is not a player. When somebody new hits in that spot, he gets his own at-bats, his own tags and his own scouting read.

  • Tap SUB in the batter strip, to the right of the hitter's name. The spot stays where it is in the order and a new, empty hitter takes it over.
  • Type his name and jersey the same way. Nothing carries over from the man he hit for, which is the point: his read should be his own.
  • A row of numbered In chips appears under the name once a spot has more than one man in it. Chip 1 is the starter, chip 2 is the first sub, and so on.
  • Re-entry: tap the earlier chip. If that spot is at the plate he gets a fresh at-bat, and every at-bat he already had stays untouched.
  • Tapped SUB by mistake? An empty sub with no name and no pitches has a small × on his chip. Tap it to take him back off.
  • The export lists each man separately and marks the sub with [in for Ortega], so the scorebook and the chart line up after the game.

Tagging a baserunner

Steals and extra bases happen while somebody else is hitting, and you don't want to lose your place.

  • Use the spot picker at the top of Batter Scouting to point the scouting box at the runner. It shows one button per spot in the order.
  • Tag him, then tap back. The at-bat, the count and the pitch log all stay exactly where they were.
  • Tapping his lineup row does something different: it makes him the hitter. Use the spot picker for this, not the lineup.

What it shows you during the game

  • Across the top: pitch count for the man on the mound, strike percentage, first-pitch strike percentage, and AO3+.
  • In the count bar: the inning, and the three out dots. Both are tappable.
  • On the Report tab, live: every arm's IP, pitches and P/IP. P/IP is the one to look at mid-game — it tells you whether he is going to finish the inning.
  • AO3+ is our stat for forcing action. An at-bat gets a plus if it ended in 3 pitches or fewer, or we were ahead after 3. It gets a second plus if it produced an out. The grid splits all 4 combinations.
  • A dropped third strike still counts as an AO3+ out, even after you tap the out dot back off. That is on purpose: the pitcher did his job and the ball got away from the catcher. AO3+ counts at-bats he finished; the dots and IP count outs that happened. The two are not meant to tie out.
  • Pitch mix shows the percentage of each pitch type that pitcher has thrown. It sits beside AO3+ on the Report tab, for the pitcher picked on the Chart tab.
  • Pitch Sequencing under the zone is the hitter's page: a plain-language read, every pitch he's seen on one grid, a table by pitch type, and each of his at-bats pitch by pitch.
  • The read line calls out where he swings and misses and where he does damage, for example misses up and away. It needs a few pitches before it says anything.

The Report tab and the export

  • The Report tab, new in v28, is the full page for the game in progress, and it is the same page a filed game opens to from History. It replaces the small box under the zone. Top to bottom: a card for every pitcher who entered (innings, pitches, batters, first-pitch strikes, strikes, free bases, strikeouts, hard contact, execution, AO3+, runs and earned) with 2 notes under his numbers; the innings; AO3+ and pitch mix; then the game note, Finish & file and Discard; and Export game at the very bottom.
  • The 2 notes. To the pitcher he will read on his game card; Coach only he never sees. Write them on the Report tab during or after the game, or tap Summary beside any game in History the morning after. A filed game's numbers can't be changed; its notes can, and a changed note goes to the dashboard the next time the app has signal (History shows notes waiting until it does).
  • Nothing to type for the numbers. On the Report tab check the inning ledger (a row per inning per pitcher) and, under it, each arm's line — IP, pitches, P/IP, runs, earned and ERA, all worked out. If an inning shows in amber it was closed without its runs; tap the row and fill it in.
  • For a file of the game, tap Export game at the bottom of the Report tab (a filed game has it at the bottom of its summary, and Export beside it in History). Then Save file to download it, or Share for the iPad share sheet — Save to Files, Mail and AirDrop are all in there. The full text is also sitting in the box if you would rather copy and paste it.
  • The file is named for the game, like pitch-chart-4-18-ponderosa.txt, so a season's worth sorts itself.
  • You get 4 things in it: readable pitcher lines, tab-separated rows for the season spreadsheet, a pitch log with one row per pitch including the zone location and the spray point, and an inning log with one row per inning per pitcher carrying runs, earned and unearned.
  • The header line records which app build produced the file.
Filing is what sends it

Finish & file keeps the game in History and sends it to the dashboard. Export is a backup, and the way to hand a file to someone directly.

Things it doesn't do

These are known. No need to write them up.

  • No score and no baserunners. Innings, outs, runs and earned runs are in — the score is not, because it needs their half of the inning too.
  • IP can read a third low. It is counted from outs the app can see, which are the ones that end an at-bat. A caught stealing, a pickoff or a runner thrown out does not get counted. The ledger shows you the outs it has, so a wrong number is visible rather than hidden — and for the record, the scorebook wins.
  • No velocity.
  • One game stored at a time, so a hitter's history resets every game.
  • Two people can't chart the same game on two iPads.
  • On the spray chart the infield is drawn to scale and the outfield is squeezed to fit the panel. Direction is accurate, depth is relative.
